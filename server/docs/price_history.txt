States

Product → Active product in stock.
ArchivedProduct → Out-of-stock or manually archived product.
ArchivedBrokenProduct → Broken product (price = 0).

Transitions

Product → ArchivedProduct → Product goes out-of-stock or is manually archived.
ArchivedProduct → Product → Restore a previously archived product.
Product → ArchivedBrokenProduct → Product price becomes 0 (broken).
ArchivedBrokenProduct → Product → Product is fixed (price > 0).
NEW → ArchivedProduct → New product that is out-of-stock but exceeds OOS threshold.
NEW → ArchivedBrokenProduct → New product with price = 0.


Price History DAG

1. Nodes
   Each `ProductPriceHistory` entry is a **node** representing the state of a product at a specific time (price, stock, etc.).
   A node points to either a `Product` (active) or an `ArchivedProduct` (out-of-stock/historical).

2. Edges / Links
   Moving a product **to archive** relinks its history nodes from `Product` → `ArchivedProduct`.
   Restoring a product relinks nodes back from `ArchivedProduct` → `Product`.
   These links **preserve historical snapshots** without creating duplicate states.

3. Linear DAG property
   Each state change creates a **new node at the end of the pipeline**, maintaining a **timeline of changes**.
   No loops: nodes always point backward to their product/archived snapshot.

4. Why this matters
   Keeps history **clean and linear**.
   Allows tracking of price/stock changes over time.
   Archives represent the last known state before a product was inactive.


Think of it like a timeline:** each node is a snapshot, active products are the current branch, 
and archived products are the “frozen branch” of past states.
